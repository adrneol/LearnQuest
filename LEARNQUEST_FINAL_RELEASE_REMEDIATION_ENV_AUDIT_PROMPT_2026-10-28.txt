### 5.1 Account deletion concurrency and zero-residue guarantee

Report 18 identified a potential race between account deletion and an in-flight write.

The user explicitly expects account deletion to remove the account and all associated learning records, sessions, metadata, action tokens, OAuth state, jobs, and auxiliary records.

Audit all account-owned data collections and asynchronous paths.

Implement a concurrency-safe deletion lifecycle:
- Atomically prevent new authenticated writes once deletion begins.
- Ensure in-flight writes cannot recreate data or write after final deletion.
- Prevent workers/retries/upserts from resurrecting an account or its learning data.
- Ensure authorization checks and state/version checks remain authoritative during mutation.
- Make cleanup idempotent and safely resumable.
- Verify every discovered account-owned collection and auxiliary record is covered.
- Make final completion mean all required data is gone, not merely that the account document disappeared.
- Avoid leaving identifiers, email addresses, notes, tokens, or other personal data in logs/tombstones after deletion.
- If any minimum non-PII anti-resurrection marker must remain temporarily, justify it and remove it under a tested lifecycle.
- Test races between deletion, data writes, sessions, and cleanup retries using isolated test databases/fixtures.

Do not delete a real user or production data as a test.

### 5.2 Import rollback and data integrity

Report 18 identified best-effort rollback behavior that can leave partial state.

Audit imports, restore, batch mutations, XP/progress updates, and all related writes.

Implement the safest approach supported by the actual schema and Atlas deployment:
- A MongoDB transaction where the real topology and operations support it; or
- A staged import with explicit validation, commit phase, idempotent journal/compensation, and visible incomplete-state handling.

Never claim atomicity if the implementation cannot guarantee it.

Ensure failed imports do not leave partial learning data, duplicate XP, or falsely report success.

Test mid-operation failures and recovery with isolated fixtures. Do not migrate or overwrite live production data.

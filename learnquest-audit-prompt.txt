Before running commands, inspect the Git status and understand the existing changes in the working tree. Preserve all pre-existing modifications.

If a command produces generated files or changes the working tree, stop and document the situation rather than attempting automatic cleanup.

Identify and verify every important calculation relating to:

- Seconds and formatted durations.
- Playlist duration totals.
- Video selection and positional ranges.
- Completed, remaining, and focused time.
- Progress percentages and checkpoint calculations.
- Playback speed and time-saving estimates.
- Efficiency metrics and eligibility conditions.
- Daily and aggregate analytics.
- Goal completion.
- XP or other ledger calculations.
- Streaks, calendar dates, and timezone handling.
- Arena scores and competitor comparisons.
- Incomplete-data flags and missing observations.
- CSV import/export calculations and validation.

For each calculation, document:

1. Its exact formula or algorithm.
2. Where its inputs originate.
3. Its expected output.
4. Its edge cases and assumptions.
5. Whether the result can be independently verified.
6. Any discrepancy between implementation, tests, documentation, and displayed UI.

Pay particular attention to timezone-safe streaks, positional range sums, incomplete playlists, efficiency denominators, eligibility rules, and aggregate Arena scores.

Do not silently change a formula because another formula seems more intuitive. Record the intended behavior supported by existing documentation and tests, and flag unresolved ambiguities.

## 13. Produce a prioritized findings register

Every material finding must have a unique identifier, such as LQ-001.

For each finding, report:

- **ID**
- **Severity:** Critical, High, Medium, Low, or Informational.
- **Category:** Correctness, UI/UX, Architecture, Performance, Data Integrity, Security, Testing, or Documentation.
- **Status:** Confirmed, Likely, Potential Risk, or Unverified.
- **Affected files and line ranges.**
- **Observed behavior or concrete evidence.**
- **Expected behavior and why it matters.**
- **Reproduction steps**, when applicable.
- **Root cause**, if established.
- **Affected features and downstream dependencies.**
- **User impact and regression risk.**
- **Recommended direction**, without implementing a solution.
- **Validation needed to confirm a fix.**

Severity guidelines:

- Critical: data loss, serious security exposure, or a core workflow that is unusable.
- High: major incorrect calculations, corrupted progress, or a significant workflow failure.
- Medium: meaningful feature defects, inconsistent state, or recurring usability problems.
- Low: limited defects or minor maintainability issues.
- Informational: improvement opportunities, architectural observations, and nonurgent cleanup.

Avoid inflating severity. Multiple symptoms caused by the same root cause should be linked rather than counted as unrelated defects.
